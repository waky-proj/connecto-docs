# Connecto off-platform ethical and safety risks

1 October 2026, Pacific/Auckland. Preliminary assessment and proposed safeguards; no policy or implementation approval is implied.

Connecto's proposed initial domain scope excludes collecting payments, holding funds, guaranteeing quality, logistics and certifying qualifications. Those boundaries do not eliminate harm arising from introductions. This assessment covers both requester and provider safety. Priorities reflect potential severity, not measured incident likelihood. Service categories, geography, member ages, insurance and safety staffing remain undecided. New Zealand is assumed for the source guidance; other markets require separate review.

| Concern | Example outside the app | Proposed safeguard and remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Violence, sexual harm and threats — critical | Assault during a home visit; coercion to finish work or pay; requester attacks a provider | Define prohibited conduct, safe exit/cancellation without rating penalties, confidential reporting and urgent restrictions. Give emergency guidance and publish actual support hours. Identity checks cannot guarantee safe conduct. |
| Stalking, theft and property access — critical | Member uses an address, copied key, access code or knowledge of an empty home after a job | Reveal precise location only when necessary; agree access arrangements; discourage sharing permanent codes; establish incident handling and restrictions covering both roles. Blocking cannot retract an address already learned. |
| Children and vulnerable adults — critical | Grooming, abuse, manipulation of someone dependent on care or unfamiliar with contracts | Proposed initial exclusion of minors and unsupervised care of vulnerable people until specialist safeguarding, category-specific checks and escalation procedures exist. Do not imply ordinary ID verification is safeguarding clearance. |
| Unsafe or unqualified work — critical | Electrical injury, dangerous construction, unsafe transport, inappropriate medical advice | Start with reviewed lower-risk categories; gate regulated or high-risk work on appropriate competence, licensing, insurance and operating processes. Ratings cannot establish professional competence. |
| Payment fraud and financial loss — high | Deposit theft, fake receipt, changed bank account, overcharging, non-payment to provider | Record currency, scope, total or pricing basis, deposit, milestones, cancellation and refund terms; require explicit approval of extras; recommend independent verification of changed bank details and traceable payments. State accurately that Connecto does not hold funds or provide payment protection. No assumed recovery guarantee. |
| Exploitation and coercion — high | Unpaid extra labour, withheld payment, sexual demands, pressure to accept unsafe work or waive rights | Permit refusal and safety-related cancellation; prohibit coercion; make reporting available despite non-completion. Review affordability and fair-treatment practices for both roles. |
| Damage, negligence and insurance gaps — high | Damaged possessions, lost keys, injury or defective work with no applicable cover | Define responsibility and claims routes; assess category-specific cover and exclusions, including platform cover, with an insurer. Do not equate an uploaded insurance document with verified current coverage. |
| Discrimination and misleading trust — high | Exclusion based on protected characteristics; new providers disadvantaged; high ratings interpreted as a safety guarantee | Publish conduct and fair-access rules; review matching and moderation outcomes; distinguish identity, credentials and review history. Explain what checks actually establish. |
| Retaliation and unfair complaint handling — high | Threats after a review; false allegations; disclosure of a reporter's address or evidence | Separate safety reports from reviews and commercial disputes; restrict access to evidence; use proportionate interim restrictions, human review and an appeal route. Do not require confrontation or bilateral completion to report. |
| Privacy and consent — high | Photos expose children, home interiors, disabilities or location; job information becomes gossip | Collect necessary information only, explain its use, define evidence access and retention, and require appropriate permission for public photos. Do not promise absolute confidentiality when lawful disclosure may be required. |
| Illegal or unethical jobs — high | Requests involving illicit goods, surveillance, harassment or exploitation | Publish allowed/prohibited categories; review ambiguous listings; define escalation and repeat-offender handling. Free-text job descriptions require operational moderation. |
| Platform incentives and accountability — high | Revenue pressure discourages suspensions; completion metrics conceal unsafe cancellations | Assign a safety owner, incident severity model, response targets matched to staffing, and auditable decisions. Track safety outcomes alongside conversion; do not penalise people for escaping unsafe engagements. |

## Proposed release gates

Before a real in-person pilot: approve eligible categories and ages; establish bilateral conduct rules and location-sharing guidance; define payment/contract boundaries; appoint safety staff and publish support coverage; operate reports independently of completion; establish severe-incident triage, proportionate restrictions, evidence controls and appeals; obtain advice on applicable consumer, privacy, health and safety obligations and insurance. A remote-only pilot still requires fraud, coercion, discrimination and privacy controls.

Serious physical danger should route people to emergency services first. In New Zealand, Police directs people to call 111 when someone is injured or in danger. Connecto reporting is not an emergency response service. Safety cases must not be treated as ordinary refund negotiation or mediated confrontation.
